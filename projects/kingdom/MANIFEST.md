# Thy Kingdom Come — Project Manifest

Updated: 2026-09-30

## 1. Vision

**Thy Kingdom Come Community Church** is a planned non-denominational ministry and community-restoration initiative designed to serve people spiritually and practically, especially people rebuilding after homelessness, rehabilitation, illness, family disruption, financial hardship or other major life transitions.

The long-term objective is not merely to create a church building. The project is intended to grow into a disciplined service ecosystem combining ministry, charitable programs, housing support, workforce pathways, food-growing projects, sustainable community development and a future physical campus.

## 3. Governance model

The project should operate with:
- A functioning board and defined officer roles.
- Adopted bylaws.
- Conflict-of-interest policy.
- Written board resolutions/minutes.
- Document-retention and recordkeeping procedures.
- Financial approval limits.
- Separate banking and accounting for each entity.
- Restricted-fund tracking for grants and designated donations.
- Written related-party agreements where any entities transact with one another.

The user has identified an initial three-director group; names and personal information should not be published in the repository unless intentionally approved for public release.

## 4. Formation sequence

1. Finalize mission, statement of faith and program purposes.
2. Confirm the best state(s) for the actual church, service nonprofit and operating activities.
3. Form the required legal entities.
4. Obtain separate EINs.
5. Adopt governance and financial-control documents.
6. Open separate entity bank accounts.
7. Implement bookkeeping/accounting categories.
8. Complete federal/state tax and charitable-solicitation compliance appropriate to each entity.
9. Establish official domain/email, donor acknowledgments and public disclosures.
10. Build the grant-ready package.
11. Launch low-cost pilot programs and partnerships.
12. Establish a real operating history and measurable outcomes.
13. Conduct property/campus due diligence.
14. Acquire/develop property only when use, infrastructure, financing and operating sustainability are verified.

## 7. Project RV / rural pilot integration

The campus concept can connect to the broader rural/off-grid project, but the legal entities and money flows must remain appropriate.

Current pilot concept:
- Approximately 5 acres.
- Rural location.
- Low-cost acquisition target.
- Water and electric available or feasible.
- Sewer preferred; workable septic acceptable.
- Practical road/highway access.
- Minimal but lawful permitting burden.
- No HOA/deed restrictions that defeat the plan.
- Zoning that can support intended RV/multiple-unit/community uses.
- Site capacity determined only after setbacks, septic, stormwater, fire access, ADA and local rules are verified.

A phased development model is preferred over building the entire campus at once.

## 9. Capital philosophy

Seed capital should primarily support:
- Entity startup.
- Due diligence.
- Professional/legal/accounting needs.
- Deposits or match/equity when appropriate.
- Reserves.
- Small pilot activity.
- Necessary infrastructure that unlocks additional financing or revenue.

Avoid exhausting all available capital on land or construction before the organization has reserves and a realistic operating plan.

## 10. Property due-diligence screen

No parcel is considered a strong acquisition until the following are checked:
- State, county and city rules.
- Zoning.
- RV use.
- Multiple dwelling/unit rules.
- Building permits and inspections.
- Licensing requirements.
- Title and liens.
- Deed restrictions / HOA / covenants.
- Legal access and easements.
- Water/well.
- Sewer/septic.
- Electric.
- Internet/communications if operationally necessary.
- Floodplain.
- Wetlands/environmental issues.
- Soil/topography.
- Stormwater.
- Fire/emergency access.
- ADA requirements.
- Realistic full site-development cost.
- Financing sources.
- Operating cash flow.
- Exit strategy.
- Worst-case downside.

## 11. Expansion model

### Phase 1 — Institution
Formation, governance, banking, controls, public identity and grant-ready documentation.

### Phase 2 — Pilot service
Low-cost outreach, community partnerships and measurable programs.

### Phase 3 — Property
Acquire land only after documented due diligence.

### Phase 4 — Initial campus
Core utilities, caretaker operations, a small number of legally permitted revenue/service uses and basic community facilities.

### Phase 5 — Program expansion
Housing/supportive services, workforce, food/agriculture, community facilities and expanded outreach.

### Phase 6 — Replication
Expand to additional locations only after the pilot demonstrates stable operations, compliance and sustainable funding.

## 12. Public-status rules

- Federal 501(c)(3) recognition has been granted.
- State charitable registration is active.
- Grants have been awarded.
- Housing programs are licensed.
- Treatment/medical programs are licensed.
- Land-use or building approvals exist.
- Public funding has been approved.
- A campus is operating.
- A specific property is secured.

Public material may describe these items as **planned**, **future**, **being organized**, **under research**, or **subject to approval**.
